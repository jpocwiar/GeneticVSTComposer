# Independent microtonal laboratory

`MicrotonalEngine.{h,cpp}` is JUCE-independent and defines its own pitch types, bank, chord sequence and objectives. It reuses `composer::generate` in **Rhythm only** mode as its rhythm planner, with the requested budget, meter, grouping, grid, phrase architecture and rhythmic character. This planner has no custom harmony or microtonal pitch genes. Microtonal pitch evolution then evaluates degree/frequency relationships independently. Shared `parseMeter`, grouping and `metricWeight` helpers keep meter semantics identical. No conventional scale snapping, harmonic prior or major/minor chord vocabulary is used for microtonal pitches. The conventional generators are unchanged.

## Pitch representation

Events store integer **degrees**, quarter-note timing and velocity. An EDO tuning stores its division count `N`, with `cents(d) = 1200*d/N` and `Hz(d) = baseHz*2^(d/N)`. A rational tuning stores ordered, reduced positive fractions in `[1, 2)`, beginning at `1/1`. For `d = octave*N + index`, `Hz(d) = baseHz*2^octave*p[index]/q[index]`. Rational steps have unequal sizes; interval scoring uses the actual frequency ratio between endpoints, rather than their degree difference alone.

Each clip owns its tuning, base frequency, duration, meter, grouping and grid. Events also carry locks; bank slots carry pins. Workspace settings configure the next search. Changing a tuning control never changes an already generated clip. The roll uses degree lanes and inspectors show EDO step notation or the exact fraction and octave, plus cents and Hz. Conventional MIDI note names do not enter this model.

## Objective

The objective combines:

- **Ratio affinity:** 75% consecutive-interval affinity (including last-to-first) and 25% affinity relative to the base. For an octave-reduced interval, search reduced fractions `p/q` with `q <= 16` and `q <= p <= 2*q`. A candidate's affinity is `exp(-0.5*(errorCents/35)^2) / (1 + log2(p*q))`; keep the largest affinity. Error is the shortest signed distance modulo 1200 cents, making the unison/octave boundary continuous. The denominator is a complexity penalty inspired by Tenney height, not a probability of sounding pleasant. EDO notes stay exactly on their grid; ratio approximations are scores and annotations, never a retuning pass.
- **Variety:** closeness of distinct degree classes to a target between two and the available distinct classes, controlled by Diversity.
- **Movement:** closeness of mean actual interval size, including the loop, to `0.04 + 0.65*motion` octaves, with extreme jumps capped at two octaves in this mean.
- **Closure:** affinity between the final and first pitches, plus a preference for ending on the base's octave class.
- **Repetition penalty:** the share of adjacent equal degrees, including the loop.

The unconditioned total is `60*consonance*affinity + 30*variety + 25*movement + 10*closure - 22*repetitionShare`, plus a metric-accent term using the shared metric weights and Syncopation. With compatible active microtonal chords, note fitness also includes affinity against the actual sounding chord voices. Scores are internal and are not calibrated across EDO systems. Affinity alone prefers unisons; the other terms counter this degeneration. The fixed 35-cent tolerance is an authored search preference.

Simple ratios are a useful starting point for harmonic spectra, but neither ratio simplicity nor this objective is a universal measure of beauty. Timbre, roughness, musical exposure and context matter. [Harrison et al., Nature Communications (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-45812-z) demonstrates effects of timbre on consonance and scale preferences. The current monitor uses a harmonic tone; the objective does not model a user's external synthesizer or perform a full psychoacoustic roughness analysis.

## Search

The independent GA uses a seeded Mersenne Twister, tournament selection, degree crossover, bounded mutation and one unchanged elite. The initial population is scored, then each generation evaluates `population - 1` new individuals: total `population + generations*(population - 1)`. Final output is the evaluated elite with its stored score. The search runs on the processor-owned search thread; only one search runs at a time. Cancellation returns no replacement sequence. A separate workspace revision prevents stale microtonal results from overwriting a new setting or restored session.

The rhythm stage uses the requested grid (eighths, sixteenths or thirty-seconds), meter, grouping, density, dynamics, rests, syncopation, arousal and weirdness through the conventional rhythm search. Its evaluations and progress are reported separately from pitch evolution. Riff and Motif additionally repeat returning pitch material before scoring. Evolution retains the source meter/length; Keep rhythm and Melody on rhythm protect timing, and locked events retain all fields. The final evaluated population supplies up to twelve alternatives; pinned slots are preserved. Bounds: 2-72 divisions or rational degrees per octave, 1-3 octaves, 20-1000 Hz base, 0.125-128 quarter-note beats, 512 events, population 8-512 and 1-2000 pitch generations. The shared rhythm planner applies its own existing minimum population of 16.

## Microtonal chords

`generateChords` searches complete polyphonic degree sequences using seeded tournament selection, crossover, mutation and one elite. Its independent controls cover length, harmonic rhythm, rhythm shape, 2-6 voices, voicing, originality, colour, brightness and final base resolution. Harmonic rhythm uses one bar, half a bar or the exact beat groups. The objective combines simultaneous pairwise ratio affinity, progression changes, voice movement, register, target span and crowding costs. Chords avoid repeated octave classes when enough classes are available, so ratio affinity cannot collapse every chord into octave doublings. No twelve-tone triad templates are used. Revoice retains timing and degree classes while changing registers. There are at most 64 chord positions and 512 events; oversized requests are rejected without replacing the existing sequence.

Chord-only generation preserves melody slots. Melody from chords and New chords + melody use the exact chord duration, meter and tuning. A pair is committed only after both searches succeed; cancellation leaves the existing pair intact. Play together repeats the shorter source over the longer duration, bounded by event capacity. It requires compatible tunings and base frequencies.

## Playback, export and persistence

`ProcessorMicrotonal.cpp` owns the independent playback path, transport, export and state conversion. The message thread publishes fixed-size playback snapshots (not the entire variation bank) under the existing short exchange lock. The audio thread only tries that lock and retains a complete previous snapshot if busy. Ratio matching and fitness never execute on the audio thread. Fifteen independent microtonal voices reuse internal voice synthesis while retaining their own emitted MIDI key and channel, so close pitches sharing a MIDI key remain independent. Oscillator increments come directly from Hz. Host meter and evolve-each-loop requests are serviced on the message thread, independently of conventional parameters.

At the output boundary only, Hz is encoded as a nearest MIDI key plus a double-precision bend calculation. Monophonic channel-1 pitch bend uses +/-2 semitones; MPE uses member channels 2-16 at +/-48 semitones. Chords/combined playback always use MPE. Approximation is limited to the 14-bit MIDI bend, roughly 0.0244 cents per step in mono and 0.5859 cents per step in MPE. Internal playback retains the exact frequency. Playback/export share independent swing, gate and deterministic humanize for melodies; voiced chord events retain their exact timings. Export sends the same setup and notes, meter/grouping and tuning annotations, and cleanup after the final note-off. No conventional scale projection or chord layer is applied.

The optional `MicrotonalWorkspace` child has extension version **2** inside the existing schema-6 document. It stores settings, twelve variations, selected sequence, pins/locks, chords and chord settings. Version 1 laboratory sessions migrate their existing sequence into slot 1 with default rhythmic/chord controls. All validation precedes the existing atomic state commit. Missing extension data restores defaults and an empty bank. Restoration cancels pending searches, stops audition, clears the separate microtonal undo/redo history and invalidates stale results. Conventional undo history and parameters remain independent.
